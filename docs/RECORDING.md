This document answers the Rule 25 question "How is recording performed?". As of 2026-10-06 the honest answer is: **recording is not designed yet.** REQ-REC-001 is `DRAFT`, and the parameters that would drive a design (container, storage medium, duration, power-loss behaviour) are undefined. *(Superseded in part 2026-10-09: owner decisions of 2026-10-08 — container MP4; storage a PCIe NVMe SSD and a USB-to-SATA HDD, with every recording mirrored to both; power-loss strategy fragmented MP4 (ADR-009, `ACCEPTED`). Still undefined: the recording duration (OQ-006) and how much may be lost on a power cut (OQ-119). The recording pipeline itself is still not designed, and REQ-REC-001 stays `DRAFT`.)* *(Superseded 2026-10-09, later: the recording duration is decided — no fixed limit, recordings run until stopped or the disk is full (owner, 2026-10-09; OQ-006 ANSWERED); one-drive-full or failure behaviour and file splitting: OQ-129. How much may be lost on a power cut is still OQ-119.)*

What this document does record:

- what is undefined and who must decide it;
- what the sources say about the building blocks (muxers, encoder behaviour, storage);
- which design questions are open;
- *(added 2026-10-09)* the owner's storage and power-loss decisions (ADR-009) and the source facts behind them (§3.1, §5.4 to §5.7, §6.1).

Fact IDs such as `[G-26]` point to [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md). `OQ-NNN` points to [OPEN_QUESTIONS.md](OPEN_QUESTIONS.md). Facts of tier `community` are worded as reports. Calculations are marked as reasoning and list their inputs.

## 2. Where recording sits in the pipeline

```text
HDMI → TC358743 → CSI-2 → CSI-2 receiver → Media Controller → V4L2 → DMABUF ─┬→ Recording encoder → Recorder (this document) ─┬→ fragmented MP4 → PCIe NVMe SSD
                                                                              │                                                └→ HDD-branch buffer (OQ-117) → fragmented MP4 → USB-to-SATA HDD (self-powered)
                                                                              └→ Live encoder ─┬→ RTMP    (STREAMING.md)
                                                                                               └→ WebRTC  (STREAMING.md)
```

*(Diagram updated 2026-10-08 after the owner answered OQ-005 with "Separate record + live". It showed one `Encoder` feeding the recorder, RTMP and WebRTC.)* Reasoning: one capture feeds two H.264 encoders, so each capture buffer has two consumers (OQ-058, OQ-115; [STREAMING.md](STREAMING.md) §2).

*(Diagram extended 2026-10-09 after ADR-009 (`ACCEPTED` 2026-10-08): the recorder's two file writers were added. The 2026-10-08 form ended at "Recorder (this document)". The "HDD-branch buffer" is a design requirement from ADR-009's Consequences, not a chosen element: OQ-117, §5.7. Reasoning: the two file writers take the recording encode's output, so the mirror adds no third consumer of capture buffers ([DMA.md](DMA.md) §9.6).)*

The recorder receives the encoder's output. *(2026-10-08: the recording encoder's output; the live encoder feeds only RTMP and WebRTC.)* The encoder differs per platform, which affects what can be recorded:

| Platform | Encoder (facts) | Consequence for recording (reasoning) |
|---|---|---|
| Pi 4 Model B, CM4 | Hardware H.264 encoder, officially specified for 1080p30 encode [D-10] | 1080p60 recording depends on unproven 1080p60 hardware encode (RISK-002, OQ-056). *(2026-10-08: the recording encode would run beside the live encode on the same hardware encoder (OQ-005). Reasoning: two 1080p30 encodes need 2 × 244,800 = 489,600 macroblocks/s, the rate of one 1080p60 encode and about 2.0× the 1080p30 specification [D-10], [D-52]. Whether the encoder sustains both: OQ-115, RISK-002.)* |
| Pi 5, CM5 | No hardware video encoder [D-31]; software encoders; official figure "H264 1080p30 encode (from ISP) ~30–40% CPU" [G-22] | If recording has its own encode, it competes for CPU with the RTMP and WebRTC encodes (RISK-003, OQ-005, OQ-059). *(Superseded 2026-10-08: recording has its own encode (owner answer to OQ-005), and RTMP and WebRTC share one live encode. Reasoning from [G-22]: two concurrent software encodes roughly double the encode CPU load. Whether it fits: OQ-059, RISK-003.)* |

No candidate platform has a hardware HEVC encoder [D-24], [D-31].

**H.265 recordings (added 2026-10-08, when H.265 was required by REQ-ENC-001; deferred — REQ-ENC-002; not in current scope).** Since the owner's answer to OQ-103 on 2026-10-08 ("H.264 only for now"), recordings are H.264 only. This part is kept as the evidence for REQ-ENC-002. Reasoning from [D-24] and [D-31]: an H.265 recording is a software encode on every candidate, CM4 included. CM4's hardware H.264 encoder [D-10] does not help it. Research topic H covered CM4 and CM5; the community benchmarks below name Pi 5 and a Pi 400.

- **Encoders.**
  - The encoder is Debian's x265 4.1-2, which Raspberry Pi does not override [H-01], [H-02].
  - FFmpeg uses it through `libx265` [H-08], [H-09]; GStreamer through `x265enc` [H-11], [H-12] (CORRECTED).
  - Both accept planar input only, not the TC358743's packed UYVY [H-10] (CORRECTED), [H-13]. Reasoning: the conversion at 1080p60 reads about 249 MB/s and writes about 187 MB/s on the CPU [H-43].
- **CM4 versus CM5.** x265's Neon DotProd kernels can apply only on CM5's Cortex-A76 [H-04], [H-05].
- **Cost evidence.**
  - A Raspberry Pi engineer stated on the official forum that software H.265 encode "is too intensive an operation to perform at any significant resolution". This is a community source; no raspberrypi.com figure was found [H-19].
  - Community benchmarks report `libx265` "Live" results of 10.00 FPS on Pi 5 and 4.33 FPS on a Pi 400 (Cortex-A72). The test is not a 1080p60 live measurement (community sources) [H-20], [H-21], [H-22].
  - Reasoning: in that harness `libx265` was about 6.6 times slower than `libx264` [H-23].
- **Throughput** on CM4 and CM5: UNKNOWN — VERIFICATION REQUIRED. HARDWARE TEST REQUIRED (OQ-104; TEST-ENC-001). RISK-022. *(2026-10-08: not in current scope. TEST-ENC-001 is now "Sustained real-time H.264 encode (H.265 deferred)"; its H.265 runs are deferred, not run in current scope, and are needed only if REQ-ENC-002 is re-activated.)*

Whether the recorder shares one encoded stream with RTMP and WebRTC, or has its own encode, is part of OQ-005. Reasoning: browser WebRTC interoperability requires H.264 Constrained Baseline [F-36], and the MediaMTX project reports that browsers do not accept H.264 B-frames [F-45]. A single shared encode would therefore carry those restrictions into the recording as well. See [STREAMING.md](STREAMING.md) and [VIDEO_ENCODER.md](VIDEO_ENCODER.md). *(Superseded 2026-10-08: decided by the owner, "Separate record + live" (answer to OQ-005): the recorder has its own encode. Reasoning: the recording therefore does not inherit these restrictions; they apply to the live encode that RTMP and WebRTC share ([STREAMING.md](STREAMING.md) §4.2). The recording encode's profile, level, B-frame use, bitrate and rate control are UNDEFINED (OQ-005). On Pi 4/CM4 the hardware encoder produces no B-frames in any case [D-14].)*

*(Added 2026-10-08.)* Reasoning from [H-33], [H-34], [H-35] and the community report [H-36], as in RISK-019: H.265 in WebRTC reaches only some browsers, so an H.264 WebRTC track has to remain for browser reach. If recordings are H.265 (OQ-103), the recording therefore cannot share the WebRTC encode. On CM5 that is a second concurrent software video encode (OQ-104, OQ-108). *(Not in current scope since 2026-10-08: OQ-103 is answered — recordings are H.264 only — so this case arises only if REQ-ENC-002 is re-activated. Whether the H.264 recording shares the H.264 WebRTC encode is still OQ-005, with the Constrained Baseline and B-frame restrictions above.)* *(2026-10-08, later: decided — it does not. The owner answered OQ-005 with "Separate record + live".)*

## 3. Container muxers available in Raspberry Pi OS and the Buildroot alternative

| Container | Raspberry Pi OS (trixie) | Buildroot 2026.08 | Notes |

### 3.1 MP4 writing modes and power loss (added 2026-10-09; research topic J; ADR-009)

ADR-009 (`ACCEPTED` 2026-10-08) chose **fragmented** MP4. The register describes three ways to write an MP4. |---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal (moov at end) | The default mode. - **Why robust muxing was not chosen** (ADR-009 Alternatives). How it, `mp4mux` or the FFmpeg MP4 muxer behave when the disk is full is not in the register: NEEDS VERIFICATION; BUILD TEST REQUIRED (OQ-129).)*
- **Fragment length.** Reasoning from [J-45] (as recorded in OQ-118): with `frag_keyframe`, the recording encode's keyframe interval sets the fragment length (§4). The fragment duration is to be set in implementation (ADR-009 decision 1): UNKNOWN — VERIFICATION REQUIRED. BUILD TEST REQUIRED (OQ-118). The amount lost on a power cut depends on it (OQ-119; §6.1).

### 5.3 Storage sizing (reasoning)

|---|---|
| 10 Mbit/s — Pi 4/CM4 encoder default [D-13] | 4.5 GB |

| 12 Mbit/s — YouTube Live's recommended 1080p60 H.265 *streaming* bitrate [H-29] (added 2026-10-08; a streaming figure, used here only as an example; H.265 deferred — REQ-ENC-002; not in current scope) | 5.4 GB |
| 17 Mbit/s — YouTube Live's recommended 1080p60 H.264 *streaming* bitrate [H-29] (added 2026-10-08; same caveat) | 7.65 GB |

### 5.5 HDD power (added 2026-10-09; research topic J; ADR-009 decision 3)

- **Example 2.5-inch drive** (the register's example, a Seagate BarraCuda ST2000LM015 / ST1000LM048 / ST500LM030 family; not a PACSCORDER choice): up to 1.0 A at +5 V during spin-up; on average 1.70 W (1-disk) or 1.80 W (2-disk) when writing; standby-to-ready 2.5 s typical and 3.0 s maximum; power-on-to-ready 2.8 / 3.3 s typical and 3.0 / 3.5 s maximum (1-disk / 2-disk); maximum sustained OD read 140 MB/s; +5 V only, through a native SATA power connector (CORRECTED) [J-30].

A self-powered enclosure or powered hub is therefore the safe choice.

### 5.7 The mirror: two file writers and HDD stalls (added 2026-10-09; ADR-009 Consequences)

ADR-009 makes every recording encode's output feed two file writers, one per drive. Its Consequences require that an HDD stall must not stall the NVMe copy or the live path; the buffering design is open (OQ-117; RISK-028).

- **Stall sources from the register.** Standby-to-ready of the example 2.5-inch HDD: 2.5 s typical, 3.0 s maximum (CORRECTED) [J-30]. UAS bridges that stop responding (community source, CORRECTED) [J-27]. On the CM4 IO Board, the HDD shares the USB 2.0 hub with every other USB device [J-06]. The register documents a forced `fsync()` after each moov rewrite in robust muxing [J-41], which ADR-009 did not choose; whether fragmented mode forces any sync is not in the register (OQ-119); `filesink`'s `o-sync` property defaults to FALSE [J-41].
- **Reasoning (from OQ-117, inputs [J-30] and [J-36]; not a design figure):** riding out a 3.0 s stall at about 3.15 MB/s per destination (25 Mbit/s video plus AAC) needs about 9.5 MB of HDD-branch buffering, before any margin and before longer stalls such as a USB reset.
- **Reasoning (from RISK-028):** if the HDD writer blocks and its branch can neither absorb nor drop, the stall propagates back to the recording encode and the NVMe copy, and, if it reaches the capture queue, into the live encode. The kernel's V4L2 documentation defines the buffer queues as FIFOs [K-36]; reasoning (the reasoning sentence of [K-36]): each filled buffer still waiting adds one frame period, 33.3 ms at 30p. A 3.0 s stall (the example drive's standby-to-ready maximum [J-30]) is three times the whole < 1 s WebRTC budget. On CM4 the recording and live encodes would also share one hardware encoder (OQ-115). Buffer-level view: [DMA.md](DMA.md) §9.6.
- **What the sources do not settle** (each UNKNOWN — VERIFICATION REQUIRED; BUILD TEST REQUIRED, HARDWARE TEST REQUIRED; OQ-117):
  - how many seconds the HDD branch buffers, and what happens when the buffer overflows (drop and mark the HDD copy incomplete, or stop the HDD copy);
  - concurrent writing to two file sinks has not been tested (research gap, topic J);
  - research suggests a large or leaky queue in front of the HDD branch, and disabling or extending spin-down where the bridge allows (research design risks, topic J — not register facts).
- The framework that implements the two writers is ADR-007 (`OPEN`); its scope note requires the HDD writer to be decoupled. Live-path queue policy: OQ-126.

### 6.1 What ADR-009 decides, and what is still lost (added 2026-10-09; research topic J)

### 7.2 Sample rate and A/V synchronisation (added 2026-10-08)

- **The recorded rate must follow the source.** Reasoning from source [I-18]:
  - No kernel path carries the HDMI sample rate into ALSA.
  - If the card is opened at 48000 Hz while the source sends 44100 Hz, the frames are labelled 48 kHz. They play 8.84 % fast and the audio timeline is 8.1 % short, so A/V drift accumulates, with no ALSA error.
  - Reasoning: a recording made that way has wrong-speed, wrong-pitch audio for its whole length.
  - RISK-023, OQ-111.
- **The driver exposes the rate.**
  - "Audio sampling rate" (ID 0x00981980, read-only) and "Audio present" (ID 0x00981981) [I-19], [I-20].
  - It sends `V4L2_EVENT_CTRL` change events [I-21].
  - Without a wired interrupt, a change takes up to about 1 s, plus I2C time, to reach the control [I-22].
  - On CM4 with legacy Unicam the controls are on `/dev/videoN`; on CM5 they are only on the TC358743 sub-device node [I-23].
- **Clock domains.**
  - The TC358743's audio PLL tracks the source's audio clock [I-26].
  - The CSI receiver drivers stamp video buffers with `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` [I-33].
  - GStreamer 1.26.2 and FFmpeg 7.1 align the two differently by default [I-35], [I-36], [I-37], [I-38].
  - Reasoning: offset at start and drift over long recordings are possible on CM4 and CM5.
  - The A/V tolerance is not set: UNKNOWN — VERIFICATION REQUIRED. OWNER DECISION REQUIRED; HARDWARE TEST REQUIRED (OQ-112, RISK-024).

## 8. Reference: how an ATEM records

ATEM switchers record on their own. This is listed as a reference for PACSCORDER's recorder design. Controlling or reading ATEM recording over the network is not in current scope: OQ-009 was answered on 2026-10-07 with HDMI capture of the ATEM output only (REQ-ATEM-001, REQ-CAP-008).

- The ATEM SDK Recording API records H.264 video and AAC audio as MP4 to an externally connected disk [F-07].
  - Its states are Idle, Recording and Stopping.
  - Its errors include `NoMedia`, `MediaFull` and `DroppingFrames` [F-07].
- ATEM Mini Pro records to USB-C media as `.mp4` H.264 with AAC audio, on ExFAT or HFS+ [F-26].
- The atem-connection project reports recording commands `RcTM` (set) and `RTMS` (status) over the network protocol [F-16] (reference only; network control is not in current scope). See [ATEM.md](ATEM.md).

Reasoning: the ATEM's error states (no media, media full, dropping frames) are a useful checklist for the status that PACSCORDER's own recorder must report. That is a design input, not a requirement. *(2026-10-09, reasoning: with no fixed duration limit (OQ-006 ANSWERED), a recording that is not stopped runs until a disk is full, so "media full" is a normal end state; and with the mirror one drive can be full, absent or failed while the other still records. What the recorder then does, and how the operator is told, is OQ-129.)*
